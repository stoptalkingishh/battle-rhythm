# Power Exercise Form References

Research scope: the seven exact `power` exercise names in `js/data/exercises.js`. Links are public viewing references only; no media was downloaded, copied, or embedded. “Visual-model takeaway” identifies the single form feature a future original illustration or animation should make unambiguous.

## Power Jump

**H2F cue cross-check:** App cues are straddle stance/arm swing, forceful jump, and soft balanced landing. ATP 7-22.02 Conditioning Drill 1 is the authoritative sequence: model the half-squat loading position, aggressive arm drive, full airborne extension, then a quiet two-foot landing with flexed hips and knees.

1. [U.S. Army H2F, “Conditioning Drill 1 Exercise 1 - Power Jump”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fy1JRb-e7Gg) (video). **Visual-model takeaway:** Establishes the Army cadence and the distinct rearward arm swing before takeoff.
2. [U.S. Army, *ATP 7-22.02: Holistic Health and Fitness Drills and Exercises*](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN40011-ATP_7-22.02-000-WEB-1.pdf), CD1. **Visual-model takeaway:** Use the doctrinal start, intermediate, and finish positions rather than a generic vertical jump.
3. [Army PRT, “Power Jump”](https://www.armyprt.com/strength_and_mobility_activities/power-jump-2.shtml). **Visual-model takeaway:** Show a straddle stance and the loading half squat, not feet-together preparation.
4. [Colorado National Guard H2F, *ATP 7-22.02 Modified Exercises and Stretches*](https://co.ng.mil/Portals/25/H2F/ATP%207-22.02%20Modified%20Exercises%20and%20Stretches.pdf). **Visual-model takeaway:** Its reduced-impact comparison makes the full version’s jump and landing excursion clear.
5. [U.S. Army H2F, “Conditioning Drill 1: Power Jump”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zuzKDJpiVBk) (video). **Visual-model takeaway:** Preserve a tall, balanced landing rather than depicting a deep collapse or a one-foot landing.

**Model baseline:** (1) Original SVG must show three key poses: loaded half squat, airborne extension, and two-foot soft landing. (2) Use motion arrows for arms rearward then forward/up and for vertical center-of-mass travel. (3) Keep both feet, knee tracking, and a neutral torso visible in the landing pose.

## Standing Long Jump

**H2F cue cross-check:** App cues are arms back, forward jump on two feet, and a stuck landing. This is a horizontal-power model: show a countermovement and arm swing feeding a two-foot takeoff, then a bilateral landing without a recovery step.

1. [National Strength and Conditioning Association standards demonstration, “Standing Long Jump”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g8-WWNp0Mnc) (video). **Visual-model takeaway:** Frame the start line and landing measurement context while keeping the jump bilateral.
2. [GymDiary, “Standing Long Jump - Exercise Guide & Instructions”](https://gym-diary.com/catalog/exercise/Standing%20Long%20Jump). **Visual-model takeaway:** Show the partial-squat countermovement and large arm swing as one continuous preparation.
3. [Gopher Sport PE Blog, “How to Use the Standing Long Jump Test in PE Classes”](https://blog.gophersport.com/standing-long-jump-test/). **Visual-model takeaway:** Emphasize a stable two-foot landing behind the measured heel mark.
4. [Oak National Academy, “Jumping for distance: standing long jump”](https://www.thenational.academy/teachers/lessons/jumping-for-distance-standing-long-jump). **Visual-model takeaway:** The visual should communicate distance direction, not a vertical tuck or high jump.
5. [Atletiq, “Standing Long Jump - how to do it, video of performing technique”](https://atletiq.com/en/exercises/1024). **Visual-model takeaway:** Use the side view to make the forward hip hinge and forward travel legible.

**Model baseline:** (1) Original SVG must depict a start line, loaded arm-back pose, forward-flight pose, and two-foot stick landing. (2) Add a single forward trajectory arrow, never a vertical-only arrow. (3) Show knees flexing over feet at landing and leave both heels visible for the “stick” cue.

## Tuck Jump

**H2F cue cross-check:** App cues are jump straight up, drive knees to chest, and land softly. ATP 7-22.02 places the movement in Conditioning Drill; visual priority is vertical takeoff and knee lift after takeoff, with a quiet bilateral landing.

1. [American Council on Exercise, “Tuck Jump”](https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/exercise-library/180/tuck-jump/). **Visual-model takeaway:** Coordinate the rearward-to-forward arm swing with a vertical jump rather than pulling knees up from a static squat.
2. [U.S. Army, *ATP 7-22.02: Holistic Health and Fitness Drills and Exercises*](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN40011-ATP_7-22.02-000-WEB-1.pdf), Conditioning Drill. **Visual-model takeaway:** Follow the Army drill’s prescribed positions and cadence when portraying the exact H2F exercise.
3. [Onnit, “Tuck Jumps: How To Do Them & Why Your Workout Needs Them”](https://www.onnit.com/blogs/the-edge/tuck-jumps-how-to-do-them-why-your-workout-needs-them). **Visual-model takeaway:** Make landing quality a first-class frame, not an omitted transition between repeated jumps.
4. [SWEAT, “Tuck Jump Exercise Instructions & Video”](https://sweat.com/exercises/tuck-jump). **Visual-model takeaway:** The knee tuck occurs in flight while the torso remains organized, not by folding forward onto the thighs.
5. [BarBend, “Tuck Jumps - Muscles Worked, Video Demo, and Exercise Benefits”](https://barbend.com/tuck-jumps/). **Visual-model takeaway:** Use a side-on midair pose with knees visibly rising toward the chest and feet beneath the body.

**Model baseline:** (1) Original SVG must include load, vertical-flight tuck, and soft landing poses. (2) Use a vertical jump arrow plus separate curved arrows from knees toward chest. (3) Keep the landing pose bilateral with flexed hips/knees; do not show knees collapsing inward.

## 8-Count T Push-Up

**H2F cue cross-check:** App cues summarize the eight-count sequence as push-up, open to T at the top, feet-to-hands, jump/clap, repeat. ATP 7-22.02 CD2 is controlling: preserve the full hand-release/T reach and the return to attention, not a rotating side-plank substitution.

1. [U.S. Army H2F, “Conditioning Drill: Eight Count T Push-Up”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Iu8E67jcXQ4) (video). **Visual-model takeaway:** Shows the complete doctrinal rhythm, including hand release and T-shaped arm reach.
2. [U.S. Army, *ATP 7-22.02: Holistic Health and Fitness Drills and Exercises*](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN40011-ATP_7-22.02-000-WEB-1.pdf), CD2, 8-Count T Push-Up. **Visual-model takeaway:** Treat the figure sequence as the canonical count-by-count pose order.
3. [Army PRT, “Conditioning Drill 2 (CD2)”](https://www.armyprt.com/h2f/conditioning-drill-2/). **Visual-model takeaway:** Distinguish the prone hand release and wide T reach from an ordinary push-up.
4. [U.S. Army, *Army Combat Fitness Test Training Guide*](https://www.army-fitness.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Official-Army-Combat-Fitness-Training-Guide.pdf), Exercise 2. **Visual-model takeaway:** Retain the slow, controlled beginner form before depicting speed or impact.
5. [USArmyBasic, “Hand Release Push Up - Everything you need to know!”](https://usarmybasic.com/hand-release-push-up-hrp/). **Visual-model takeaway:** Its written count sequence supports labeling the squat, thrust, prone, T, return, and jump phases correctly.

**Model baseline:** (1) Original SVG must use an eight-step or clearly numbered multi-pose sequence, not one composite pose. (2) Include a prone hand-release frame with arms extended to a true lateral T. (3) Include the feet-to-hands and vertical jump/clap frames, with directional arrows showing the transition order.

## Kettlebell Swing

**H2F cue cross-check:** App cues are hinge rather than squat, snap hips forward, and swing to chest height with relaxed arms. The model must make the hinge and hip-driven bell path more prominent than arm lifting.

1. [American Council on Exercise, “The ACE Do It Better Series: The Two-handed Kettlebell Swing”](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/january-2025/8788/the-ace-do-it-better-series-the-two-handed-kettlebell-swing/). **Visual-model takeaway:** Model the swing as an explosive hinge, not a squat-to-front-raise.
2. [StrongFirst, “The Kur for Your Kettlebell Swing and Clean”](https://www.strongfirst.com/the-kur-for-your-kettlebell-swing-and-clean/). **Visual-model takeaway:** Use a deadlift-like hinge as the visual foundation for the backswing.
3. [StrongFirst, “Improve Your Kettlebell Swing”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PAhDt_0PjP4) (video). **Visual-model takeaway:** Show a crisp standing lockout with hips and knees extended before the bell descends.
4. [American Council on Exercise, “Kettlebells: Mastering the Swing”](https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/course/4a5wzxxzz/kettlebells-mastering-the-swing/). **Visual-model takeaway:** Depict the bell as a consequence of hip extension, with arms functioning as a connection rather than an active raise.
5. [StrongFirst, “Kettlebell Swing Demonstration”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAgKPTKp6PI) (video). **Visual-model takeaway:** Use the side view to show the bell passing high between the thighs on the hinge, with a neutral spine.

**Model baseline:** (1) Original SVG must show a side-view hinge/bell-back pose and a tall lockout with bell approximately chest height. (2) Add a hip-extension arrow and a pendulum arc for the bell; do not add an arm-curl arrow. (3) Keep a neutral trunk line, shins near vertical in the hinge, and relaxed straight arms visible.

## Sled Push / Sled Drag

**H2F cue cross-check:** App cues are whole-body drive, chest up, and constant-effort push; safety requires a clear lane, two-hand grip, and secure footing. Cover both directions because the named exercise contains both: push with a forward lean and drag with controlled backward travel or a harness/strap configuration.

1. [SWEAT, “Sled Push Exercise Instructions & Video”](https://sweat.com/exercises/sled-push). **Visual-model takeaway:** Show locked hands on the uprights, a braced trunk, and continuous forward steps rather than a shove-and-coast.
2. [Women’s Health UK, “How to sled push properly: Form tips, mistakes, muscles worked”](https://www.womenshealthmag.com/uk/fitness/strength-training/a60657649/sled-push/). **Visual-model takeaway:** Establish the body-to-sled lean and long, forceful ground-contact position.
3. [Fitbod, “Sled Push: Form, Muscles Worked, Target Weight & Variations”](https://fitbod.me/exercises/sled-push). **Visual-model takeaway:** The demonstration supports a low, stable pushing posture with the entire body driving the sled.
4. [The Workout Magazine, “Sled Push and Pull Form: Variations and Progressions”](https://theworkoutmag.com/exercise-howto/sled-push-pull-form-progressions). **Visual-model takeaway:** Use a separate drag panel so backward/strap work is not visually conflated with the push.
5. [YouTube, “Sled Push | Proper Form Tutorial for Power & Conditioning”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YJbKlXj4WhI) (video). **Visual-model takeaway:** Show constant lane-direction travel and feet striking securely beneath a rigid torso.

**Model baseline:** (1) Original SVG must have separate labeled push and drag panels sharing a clearly marked lane direction. (2) In push, show both hands on sled uprights, straight arm force line, and a forward body lean; in drag, show the chosen strap/handhold and backward travel. (3) Include foot-placement/ground-contact arrows and no surrounding obstacles or athletes in the lane.

## Overhead Push-Press (power variation)

**H2F cue cross-check:** App cues are fast dip-drive and lockout; safety requires a braced core and no low-back hyperextension. The model should distinguish the power variation from a strict press by visibly using a shallow knee/hip dip followed by a fast, vertical leg drive.

1. [U.S. Army, *ATP 7-22.02: Holistic Health and Fitness Drills and Exercises*](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN40011-ATP_7-22.02-000-WEB-1.pdf), Free Weight Core. **Visual-model takeaway:** Use the Army Free Weight Core implementation as the primary H2F reference for the exact movement family.
2. [CrossFit, “The Push Press - Instruction, Benefits & Technique”](https://www.crossfit.com/essentials/the-push-press). **Visual-model takeaway:** Make the dip upright and short, then show the legs extending before/with the press.
3. [Catalyst Athletics, “Push Press | Olympic Weightlifting Exercise Library”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yklSQG1_Ovc) (video). **Visual-model takeaway:** Use the bar’s vertical path and the timing handoff from leg drive to arms.
4. [Muscle & Fitness, “Push Press Exercise: How To, Benefits, Variations”](https://www.muscleandfitness.com/workouts/full-body-exercises/push-press-exercise-how-to-benefits-and-variations/). **Visual-model takeaway:** Preserve a stacked finish: bar over shoulder/hip/mid-foot rather than behind the head with lumbar arch.
5. [Torokhtiy Weightlifting, “Push Press”](https://torokhtiy.com/blogs/guides/push-press). **Visual-model takeaway:** The side-view progression clarifies the bar starts in the front rack and finishes at full overhead lockout.

**Model baseline:** (1) Original SVG must show front-rack start, shallow vertical dip, leg-drive/bar-rise, and overhead lockout. (2) Add one vertical bar-path arrow and a separate downward/upward arrow at the knees to distinguish dip from strict pressing. (3) At lockout, show elbows straight, bar stacked over mid-foot, ribs down, and a neutral lumbar curve.
