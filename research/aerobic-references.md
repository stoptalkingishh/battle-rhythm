# Aerobic-Endurance Visual Reference Research

Scope: the seven `aerobic-endurance` entries in `js/data/exercises.js`. Sources are ordered with Army/H2F/AFT material first where it addresses the activity, followed by public military, medical, coaching, or peer-reviewed education. “Visual-model takeaway” is a design interpretation of the cited guidance, not a claim that a source supplies an image. No media is included or downloaded here.

## Unit Formation Run (UFR)

**H2F cue cross-check:** The exercise data calls for holding formation and pace while breathing rhythmically and staying relaxed. FM 7-22 supports unit-run programming; drill-and-ceremony references are useful for the formation, alignment, and leader-facing portion, while running references support the relaxed posture cue.

1. **[FM 7-22, Holistic Health and Fitness](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN44522-FM_7-22-002-WEB-7.pdf)** — Department of the Army. Aerobic-endurance programming identifies unit formation runs as a collective endurance method. **Visual-model takeaway:** show a compact running element moving at one deliberately uniform effort, not a set of independent sprinting poses.
2. **[ATP 3-21.5, Drill and Ceremonies](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/pdf/web/atp3_21x5.pdf)** — Department of the Army. Establishes alignment, interval, and leader-controlled movement conventions. **Visual-model takeaway:** use parallel ranks/files, a visible lead/guide position, and consistent lateral spacing.
3. **[DAFMAN 36-2905, Department of the Air Force Physical Fitness Program](https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/af_a1/publication/dafman36-2905/dafman36-2905.pdf)** — Department of the Air Force. Provides official service fitness-run context and test-running controls. **Visual-model takeaway:** runners retain a tall trunk and forward gaze while sharing a controlled route direction.
4. **[MCO 6100.13A, Marine Corps Physical Fitness Program](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/MCO%206100.13A.pdf)** — U.S. Marine Corps. Service physical-fitness policy includes organized conditioning and running assessment context. **Visual-model takeaway:** depict disciplined, repeatable movement rather than exaggerated race-day form.
5. **[The biomechanics of running](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0966-6362(97)00038-6)** — *Gait & Posture*, Novacheck. Peer-reviewed overview of running gait mechanics. **Visual-model takeaway:** represent a slight whole-body forward lean from the ankles, neutral head, and alternating arm-leg swing without shoulder elevation.

**Model baseline:**
1. Original SVG requirement: side-plus-plan view of 8–12 uniformed silhouettes in two aligned files, with equal gaps and a clearly designated formation leader.
2. Original SVG requirement: leader and runners use upright heads, relaxed bent arms, and a modest ankle-led lean; avoid bowed heads, rigid elbows, or a marching high-knee gait.
3. Original SVG requirement: include unobtrusive pace arrows shared by every runner and a route/road-edge safety boundary; do not portray a finish-line sprint.

## Ability Group Run (AGR)

**H2F cue cross-check:** The data specifies running with an ability group at controlled effort. The visual distinction from UFR should be matching runners by pace band, not depicting a strict drill formation or a race.

1. **[FM 7-22, Holistic Health and Fitness](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN44522-FM_7-22-002-WEB-7.pdf)** — Department of the Army. Frames aerobic training intensity and endurance development within H2F programming. **Visual-model takeaway:** label the group as a steady aerobic effort rather than an all-out test effort.
2. **[H2F: Holistic Health and Fitness](https://www.army.mil/h2f/)** — U.S. Army. Official H2F overview centers training on readiness and individual performance needs. **Visual-model takeaway:** separate pace groups by visual band/color/label while keeping each group internally cohesive.
3. **[Physical Readiness Program Guide 5](https://www.mynavyhr.navy.mil/Portals/55/Support/Culture%20Resilience/Physical/Guide_5-Physical-Readiness-Program.pdf)** — U.S. Navy. Public Navy physical-readiness guidance covers aerobic conditioning and paced running. **Visual-model takeaway:** show a sustainable, repeatable stride and conversational-scale exertion rather than one runner pulling away.
4. **[ACSM's Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription](https://www.acsm.org/education-resources/books/guidelines-exercise-testing-prescription)** — American College of Sports Medicine. Authoritative exercise-prescription resource for intensity selection and progression. **Visual-model takeaway:** pair each group with a distinct effort/pace label, preserving similar body language within the group.
5. **[The Talk Test as a Marker of Exercise Intensity](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21297594/)** — *Journal of Cardiopulmonary Rehabilitation and Prevention*. Peer-reviewed evidence on speech comfort as an intensity marker. **Visual-model takeaway:** use a small optional speech icon for a controlled group, not a gasping or maximal-effort expression.

**Model baseline:**
1. Original SVG requirement: three small groups on the same route, each carrying a plain pace-band tag such as A, B, or C; do not imply rank or performance judgment.
2. Original SVG requirement: preserve matched stride length and spacing inside each group while allowing visible distance between groups.
3. Original SVG requirement: render neutral, relaxed shoulders and level heads, with a small conversational-effort indicator only for the controlled group.

## Release Run (RR)

**H2F cue cross-check:** The data directs a common start, a conversational effort, pace control, and compliance with a pre-briefed route. The model should emphasize self-selected steady effort after release, not formation alignment.

1. **[FM 7-22, Holistic Health and Fitness](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN44522-FM_7-22-002-WEB-7.pdf)** — Department of the Army. H2F endurance guidance supports appropriately controlled, progressive aerobic work. **Visual-model takeaway:** identify RR as an easy-to-moderate endurance run with no single imposed formation pace.
2. **[DoDI 1308.03, DoD Physical Fitness/Body Composition Program](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/130803p.pdf)** — Department of Defense. Establishes department-wide military physical-fitness program policy. **Visual-model takeaway:** depict a supervised, planned activity with a clear route rather than an unbounded individual run.
3. **[Guide 5: Physical Readiness Program](https://www.mynavyhr.navy.mil/Portals/55/Support/Culture%20Resilience/Physical/Guide_5-Physical-Readiness-Program.pdf)** — U.S. Navy. Public military fitness education on aerobic training progression. **Visual-model takeaway:** use a comfortably repeatable cadence and a route marker instead of a stopwatch-driven sprint visual.
4. **[Running: How to get started](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/fitness/in-depth/running/art-20050900)** — Mayo Clinic. Clinician-reviewed introduction to safe, progressive running. **Visual-model takeaway:** model relaxed arms and posture, gradual effort, and a visible return/route plan.
5. **[Physical Activity Basics](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/about/index.html)** — Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Public-health guidance distinguishes moderate from vigorous activity and supports regular aerobic work. **Visual-model takeaway:** favor even, non-strained silhouettes and an effort legend rather than competitive overtaking.

**Model baseline:**
1. Original SVG requirement: start cluster dispersing into individual or pairs on one signed loop, with direction arrows that visibly return to the start/control point.
2. Original SVG requirement: each runner retains the same tall, relaxed posture, but spacing becomes intentionally nonuniform after release.
3. Original SVG requirement: include a route boundary/checkpoint icon and a conversational-effort legend; exclude racing bibs, podiums, and finish-line poses.

## Foot March / Ruck March (FM)

**H2F cue cross-check:** The data’s load-high-and-tight, natural-stride, foot-care, and pace-discipline cues are directly supported by load-carriage guidance. The visual must make pack adjustment and conservative progression more prominent than speed.

1. **[FM 7-22, Holistic Health and Fitness](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN44522-FM_7-22-002-WEB-7.pdf)** — Department of the Army. Foot-march programming includes progressive manipulation of load, distance, speed, and grade and cautions with heavy relative loads. **Visual-model takeaway:** show one progression variable changing at a time and a deliberately sustainable marching pace.
2. **[ATP 3-21.18, Foot Marches](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN38170-ATP_3-21.18-000-WEB-1.pdf)** — Department of the Army. Army tactical publication for planning and conducting foot marches. **Visual-model takeaway:** use a load-carriage side profile, route/terrain information, and a controlled march column.
3. **[Load Carriage in Military Operations: A Review of Historical, Physiological, Biomechanical, and Medical Aspects](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3445177/)** — Knapik, Reynolds, and Harman, *Military Medicine*. Peer-reviewed review of military load carriage. **Visual-model takeaway:** keep the load close to the back and show a stable trunk rather than a backward-leaning, loosely hanging pack.
4. **[How to Fit and Size a Backpack](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/backpacking-pack.html)** — REI Co-op Expert Advice. Public equipment-fitting instruction explains hip-belt, shoulder-strap, load-lifter, and sternum-strap adjustment. **Visual-model takeaway:** label the hip belt over the iliac crest, snug shoulder straps, and load lifters drawing the pack high and close.
5. **[Blisters](https://www.footcaremd.org/foot-and-ankle-conditions/toenails/blisters)** — FootCareMD, American Orthopaedic Foot & Ankle Society. Clinical patient education describes friction blisters and prevention considerations. **Visual-model takeaway:** include a separate foot-care inspection inset, clearly distinguished from gait mechanics, and do not imply that pain should be ignored.

**Model baseline:**
1. Original SVG requirement: side-view marcher with ruck mass high against the upper back, hip belt centered on the pelvis, sternum strap, and labeled load-lifter angle.
2. Original SVG requirement: demonstrate a natural walking stride with head level and slight forward body angle, not a deep forward bend or overstriding heel reach.
3. Original SVG requirement: add a small progression panel that changes only one of load, distance, speed, or grade, plus a separate boot/sock/foot-check inset.

## 2-Mile Run

**H2F cue cross-check:** The source data specifies a steady start, negative splits, relaxed upper body, and a measured flat test course. A visual should show pace management across two miles, not merely a generic runner or a maximal opening sprint.

1. **[Army Fitness Test](https://www.army.mil/aft/)** — U.S. Army. Official AFT information identifies the two-mile run as the aerobic-endurance event. **Visual-model takeaway:** use a measured-course and timekeeping context, clearly separated from everyday training graphics.
2. **[FM 7-22, Holistic Health and Fitness](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN44522-FM_7-22-002-WEB-7.pdf)** — Department of the Army. Provides the Army endurance-training framework that prepares Soldiers for aerobic assessment. **Visual-model takeaway:** show repeatable pacing and recovery-aware training rather than a test attempt every session.
3. **[DAFMAN 36-2905, Department of the Air Force Physical Fitness Program](https://static.e-publishing.af.mil/production/1/af_a1/publication/dafman36-2905/dafman36-2905.pdf)** — Department of the Air Force. Official fitness program guidance supplies standardized timed-run context. **Visual-model takeaway:** depict a defined course and timer with a stable, legal running line rather than an obstacle course.
4. **[Pacing Strategy for Optimal Performance in Endurance Events](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17953462/)** — Tucker and Noakes, *Sports Medicine*. Peer-reviewed review of endurance pacing. **Visual-model takeaway:** use a two-segment pace bar that begins controlled and becomes modestly faster only late in the effort.
5. **[Effects of Step Rate Manipulation on Joint Mechanics During Running](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181ebedf4)** — Heiderscheit et al., *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*. Peer-reviewed study of cadence changes and running mechanics. **Visual-model takeaway:** model compact steps landing near the body, with relaxed arm swing, rather than a long reaching stride.

**Model baseline:**
1. Original SVG requirement: measured, flat two-mile loop or out-and-back with start, turnaround if applicable, finish, and timer icons; label it “test course.”
2. Original SVG requirement: a two-part pace strip depicts controlled first mile and slightly quicker second mile, with no exact pace prescribed.
3. Original SVG requirement: side silhouette has level head, relaxed shoulders, elbows bent near 90 degrees, and foot contact near the center of mass.

## Sustained Run

**H2F cue cross-check:** The data calls for constant pace, controlled breathing, and progressive distance. It aligns with continuous aerobic conditioning, so the visual must make duration and steady effort legible without implying test pace.

1. **[FM 7-22, Holistic Health and Fitness](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN44522-FM_7-22-002-WEB-7.pdf)** — Department of the Army. H2F provides endurance-development and progression guidance. **Visual-model takeaway:** represent a continuous, stable effort line across the whole session.
2. **[DoDI 1308.03, DoD Physical Fitness/Body Composition Program](https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/DD/issuances/dodi/130803p.pdf)** — Department of Defense. DoD policy places regular physical conditioning in readiness programming. **Visual-model takeaway:** frame the run as planned recurring training, not a one-off maximal challenge.
3. **[Aerobic Exercise](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/adding-adults/aerobic-activity.html)** — Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Explains continuous aerobic activity and intensity categories. **Visual-model takeaway:** use a duration ring or steady effort zone rather than interval work/rest blocks.
4. **[Running Form](https://www.hss.edu/health-library/move-better/running-form)** — Hospital for Special Surgery. Sports-medicine education on running posture and efficient mechanics. **Visual-model takeaway:** show gaze ahead, low relaxed shoulders, a stable torso, and arm swing traveling forward/back rather than crossing the body.
5. **[The influence of stride frequency and length on running mechanics](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27941482/)** — Schubert, Kempf, and Heiderscheit, *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*. Peer-reviewed running-mechanics research. **Visual-model takeaway:** avoid an extreme stride; use a compact, repeatable gait suited to sustained pace.

**Model baseline:**
1. Original SVG requirement: one runner follows a simple continuous route beside a constant-effort line and a duration range, with no interval peaks.
2. Original SVG requirement: front and side posture callouts show eyes forward, head stacked over trunk, low shoulders, and elbows driving behind the body.
3. Original SVG requirement: render even footfalls and an optional controlled-breathing cadence mark; prohibit exaggerated chest-up arching or shoulder shrugging.

## Endurance Training Machine (ETM) Session

**H2F cue cross-check:** The data requires a 30–45 minute session in Zones 1–3 with heart-rate monitoring and hydration. It does not name a machine, so the visual should be machine-neutral unless a specific modality is selected.

1. **[FM 7-22, Holistic Health and Fitness](https://armypubs.army.mil/epubs/DR_pubs/DR_a/ARN44522-FM_7-22-002-WEB-7.pdf)** — Department of the Army. H2F endurance schedules include endurance-training-machine work and heart-rate-zone programming. **Visual-model takeaway:** make the target zone and continuous duration the primary visual controls.
2. **[H2F: Holistic Health and Fitness](https://www.army.mil/h2f/)** — U.S. Army. Official H2F resource supports readiness-oriented, individualized training. **Visual-model takeaway:** provide interchangeable bike, rower, and elliptical silhouettes under the same zone target instead of treating one device as mandatory.
3. **[Target Heart Rates](https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/fitness/fitness-basics/target-heart-rates)** — American Heart Association. Public clinical education explains using target heart-rate ranges to gauge exercise intensity. **Visual-model takeaway:** display a heart-rate gauge with an explicitly highlighted target band, not a redline/maximal zone.
4. **[Rating of Perceived Exertion (RPE) Scale](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/measuring/index.html)** — Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Public-health explanation of monitoring intensity by perceived effort. **Visual-model takeaway:** pair the zone gauge with a simple effort check so the model is not dependent on a wearable display.
5. **[Quantity and Quality of Exercise for Developing and Maintaining Cardiorespiratory Fitness](https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0b013e3180616b27)** — American College of Sports Medicine position stand, *Medicine & Science in Sports & Exercise*. Authoritative exercise prescription for aerobic frequency, intensity, and duration. **Visual-model takeaway:** show cadence/resistance as adjustable controls that preserve the selected intensity band.

**Model baseline:**
1. Original SVG requirement: modular bike, rower, and elliptical silhouettes with one shared 30–45 minute zone panel; do not label a generic ETM as a treadmill.
2. Original SVG requirement: show neutral spine, relaxed shoulders, and correctly directed hand/foot contact for each machine, with no locked elbows or rounded-back rower pose.
3. Original SVG requirement: include a heart-rate-zone gauge, RPE check, water bottle icon, and a clear instruction that resistance/cadence changes are used to stay in the target band.
